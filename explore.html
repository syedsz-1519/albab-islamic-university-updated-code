Five Pillars Foundation

The core practices of Islam.

ٱلشَّهَادَةُ — The Testimony of Faith

The rules and conditions

To declare: "There is no deity except Allah, and Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah." This declaration must be made with sincere heart, belief, and full understanding.

The practical steps

Speak the testimony with full conviction. In Islam, reciting the shahada with genuine belief and intention is the formal entry into the faith.

Spiritual significance

The shahada is the foundation of all Islamic belief. It affirms absolute monotheism and recognition of Muhammad's prophethood.

Scripture proof

"There is no deity except Allah" — Qur'an 47:19

ٱلصَّلَاةُ — The Prayer

The rules and conditions

Five daily prayers: Fajr (dawn, before sunrise), Dhuhr (just after noon), Asr (late afternoon), Maghrib (immediately after sunset), Isha (dark night). Each prayer requires ritual purity (wudu), facing the Qibla (Mecca), and specific movements.

The practical steps

  1. Perform wudu (ritual ablution)
  2. Stand facing the Qibla
  3. Make the intention (niyyah)
  4. Perform the prescribed postures: standing, bowing, prostration, sitting
  5. Complete with the taslim (greeting of peace)

Spiritual significance

Prayer is a direct connection to Allah, a moment of reflection, and a foundation for righteous living. It disciplines the soul and reminds believers of their purpose.

ٱلزَّكَاةُ — The Purifying Alms

The rules and conditions

2.5% of eligible wealth held for a full lunar year above the Nisab:

  • Gold: 87.48 grams (approximately 3 ounces)
  • Silver: 612.36 grams (approximately 22 ounces)

Zakat purifies wealth and spirit, supporting the poor and vulnerable.

The practical steps

  1. Calculate eligible wealth (cash, gold, silver, investments)
  2. Determine the nisab threshold in your currency
  3. Pay 2.5% of qualifying wealth
  4. Distribute to eligible recipients (the poor, needy, debtors, travelers, etc.)

Spiritual significance

Zakat is a pillar of social justice, fostering compassion and equity within the community.

ٱلصَّوْمُ — The Fasting

The rules and conditions

During Ramadan: Abstain from food, drink, and intimate relations from dawn (Fajr) to sunset (Maghrib).

Exemptions: Children, travelers, the sick, the elderly, pregnant or menstruating women, and those with legitimate hardship.

The practical steps

  1. Make the intention (niyyah) to fast before Fajr
  2. Abstain from food and drink
  3. Maintain moral discipline and avoid arguments
  4. Break the fast (iftar) at sunset with dates and water, traditionally
  5. Make up missed fasts after Ramadan if needed

Spiritual significance

Fasting develops self-control, empathy for the poor, and spiritual focus. It is a month of heightened awareness and divine closeness.

Scripture proof

"O you who believe, fasting is prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, that you might become conscious of Allah." — Qur'an 2:183

ٱلْحَجُّ — The Pilgrimage

The rules and conditions

Once in a lifetime for those with health, finances, and means. Performed during Dhul-Hijjah (the 12th Islamic month).

Requirements: physical fitness, financial capacity, and safe passage.

The practical steps

  1. Enter Ihram (sacred pilgrimage state) with intention
  2. Perform Tawaf (seven circuits around the Kaaba)
  3. Walk between Safa and Marwa (Sa'y) seven times
  4. Stand at Arafat for spiritual reflection
  5. Pelt pebbles at the Jamarat (symbolic stoning of evil)
  6. Perform the farewell Tawaf before departure

Spiritual significance

Hajj is the physical and spiritual culmination of Islamic practice. Pilgrims from around the world unite in worship, experiencing profound spiritual transformation and global Islamic unity.
